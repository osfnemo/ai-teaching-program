What an AI harness is

The model is the brain. The harness is the body: the app, the memory, the tools, and the permissions that let the brain actually do things for you.

Facilitator (0:25 to 0:35): Keep this conceptual and short. The point is to make "Claude", "Claude Code" and "Codex" stop sounding like different products and start sounding like different bodies for the same kind of brain. Show the desktop app live for 60 seconds. Do not open a terminal today.
The modelClaude, GPT, Gemini. The thing that reads, reasons and writes. On its own it can only talk.
→
The harnessThe app around the model. Gives it memory, files, tools, and rules about what it may do.
→
Your worldYour Drive, your email, your website, your spreadsheets. Reached through connectors.

Words you will hear today

Model
The AI itself. Different models trade speed and cost against depth of reasoning.
Harness
The app that wraps the model and gives it tools and memory.
Connector
A permission that lets the AI read from or act in another service, such as Google Drive.
Project
A folder inside Claude with its own standing instructions and reference files.
System prompt
Standing instructions the AI reads before every conversation. In Claude, "project instructions".
Context
Everything the AI can see right now: your message, files, instructions, earlier turns.
Effort / thinking
How long the model reasons before answering. More is slower, more careful, and costs more.
Deploy
Putting a website or tool online so others can use it.

Section 4

Connectors: Google Drive

A connector is a permission slip. You tell Claude "you may look at my Drive", and from then on you can ask questions about your own documents without copying and pasting.

Facilitator (0:50 to 1:05, then BREAK at 1:05): This is the moment of the lesson. Get one real file of theirs summarised by Claude before the break. Point out the Google consent screen and explain what "read only" means. If Drive is slow to index, ask them to search for a file by name first. After the break resume at Section 5.

What a connector actually does

  • You approve a link between Claude and a service (Google Drive, Gmail, Calendar, Slack, and many more).
  • Claude can then search and read from that service when you ask, inside a conversation.
  • You can disconnect it at any time in Settings. Most connectors are read-only unless you allow more.
  • Claude does not sit reading your Drive in the background. It looks only when you ask a question that needs it.

Take care. Connecting Drive means anything in that Drive can be read into a conversation if you ask. If your Drive holds client records, payroll or contracts, we will talk in Section 11 about what to ask and what to keep out.

Section 5

Projects and standing instructions

A project is a folder inside Claude that remembers who you are. Its instructions are read before every conversation, so you stop repeating yourself. This is what people mean by "tuning the system prompt".

Facilitator (1:15 to 1:30): Use the generator below. It drafts project instructions from their Section 1 answers. Have them copy it into a new project, then ask the same question they asked in Section 3 and compare the answers side by side. That contrast teaches the value of instructions better than any explanation.

What good instructions contain

  1. Who you are: the business, what it sells, who buys it.
  2. How you sound: tone, words you use, words you never use.
  3. What you want by default: length, format, plain English, ask before assuming.
  4. What to avoid: no invented facts, no legal or tax advice presented as certain, no jargon.
  5. Reference files: your price list, a good past email, your services page. Add these to the project's knowledge.

Section 6

Choosing a model and how hard it thinks

You do not need the strongest model for everything. Match the tool to the job, the way you would not hire a barrister to proofread a flyer.

Facilitator (1:30 to 1:40): Show the model picker at the top of a chat and the extended thinking toggle. Rule of thumb for them: Sonnet as the daily default, step up to Opus or Fable when the answer matters or the task is long and messy, step down to Haiku for quick reformatting. Do not go deeper than this today.
ModelThink of it asUse it for
HaikuFast assistantQuick rewrites, summaries, sorting lists, simple questions. Cheapest and fastest.
SonnetCapable colleagueEveryday writing, emails, planning, analysis. Your default.
OpusSenior specialistLong documents, tricky decisions, multi-step research, anything you would double-check with an expert.
FableThe best availableHardest problems, building software with Claude Code, work where mistakes are expensive.

Effort and extended thinking

Most harnesses let you choose how long the model reasons before it answers. Low effort is instant and fine for simple asks. High effort or "extended thinking" makes the model plan and check itself. It is slower and uses more of your allowance, so save it for tasks where being right matters more than being quick.

Simple rule: start with Sonnet and normal effort. If the answer is shallow or wrong, step up one level and ask again. If it is over-long and slow for a trivial task, step down.

Decide for your own tasks

Pick a model and effort for each. There is no single right answer, the point is to practise the judgement.
